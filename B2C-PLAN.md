# NARI: first customers and a path to profitability

Working proposal, 4 October 2026. Starting point supplied by the founder:
3–4 customers, no marketing yet. Clinician fees, acquisition costs, repeat use
and conversion rates are not known. The targets below are experiments, not
forecasts or industry benchmarks. No offer changes in this document have been
applied to the website.

## First objective

Find one service that 20 paying customers value, can be delivered reliably, and
leaves money after direct costs. Start in one city where the team can actually
provide care. Women's physiotherapy follow-up is a candidate given the current
website; choose it only if clinician availability and customer interviews
support it. A broad promise covering every women's health concern is harder to
explain and operate at this stage.

Speak personally to all 3–4 customers. Ask what triggered their search, how they
found NARI, what nearly stopped the booking, what alternative they considered,
what happened after the consultation, and what would make them return or
recommend it. Record their words and permission to contact them again.

## Fix the offer economics before advertising it

The current site sells ₹199 single consultations, unlimited ₹799 weekly care
and unlimited ₹2,499 monthly care. If appointments are otherwise comparable:

- Four single consultations cost ₹796, less than the weekly membership.
- Twelve single consultations cost ₹2,388, less than the monthly membership.
- Unlimited becomes attractive mainly with heavier use, increasing clinician
  costs. Priority or follow-up support could justify a premium, but its value
  and delivery cost must be measured.

My recommendation is to pilot explicitly defined packages with clinician-agreed
session counts, duration, follow-up access and response hours. For example,
a weekly package might include one consultation plus a scheduled check-in; a
monthly package might include two consultations plus clearly scoped follow-ups.
These are product hypotheses, not clinical care recommendations. A clinician
must decide what care is appropriate. Do not reduce benefits already purchased.

Keep ₹199 only if its scope and actual provider cost make sense. If it is a
subsidized introduction, record that subsidy as an acquisition cost. Do not
assume a full specialist visit can profitably be delivered at that price.

For every paying customer calculate:

`Contribution = collected revenue after refunds − clinician payouts − coordination/support cost − payment fees − variable tools/consumables − acquisition cost`

Value founder support time too. Contribution is not net profit: fixed salaries,
software, premises, other overheads and applicable taxes still need coverage.

Illustration only: assume ₹300 per clinician session, ₹200 coordination and
₹50 payment/tool costs per monthly member. Replace these inputs with invoices.

| Monthly usage | Revenue | Direct delivery cost | Contribution before marketing and fixed costs |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 sessions | ₹2,499 | ₹850 | ₹1,649 |
| 4 sessions | ₹2,499 | ₹1,450 | ₹1,049 |
| 8 sessions | ₹2,499 | ₹2,650 | −₹151 |

At four sessions and an illustrative ₹300 customer acquisition cost, only ₹749
remains before fixed costs. At eight sessions, more traffic would grow losses.
Break-even customer count is fixed monthly costs divided by positive average
contribution after acquisition, rounded up; use actual retained revenue and
usage, not a headline membership price.

## Build trust before buying clicks

The website currently says “5,000+ women”, “50+ experts”, “98% satisfaction” and
“24×7 support”. Verify these against records. If they do not describe the
current business, replace them with truthful early-stage promises such as
named available clinicians, clear appointment durations and actual support
hours. Publish only genuine, consented testimonials.

Restore the original clinician photos, show verified qualifications and
registration details where applicable, explain who provides the consultation,
and show cancellation/refund terms and appointment availability before payment.
Google's guidance emphasizes expertise, sourcing and trust, particularly for
health content: [helpful, reliable content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content).

## A 30-day customer experiment

| Period | Work | Evidence to collect |
|---|---|---|
| Days 1–7 | Interview existing customers; agree clinician costs and slots; choose one service; correct unsubstantiated claims. | Actual problem, objections, cost per session, promised support hours. |
| Days 8–14 | Publish one clear offer; personally follow up with interested prospects; ask satisfied customers for introductions; arrange educational introductions through a few relevant local communities with permission. | Qualified enquiries, bookings, completed appointments, reasons people decline. |
| Days 15–21 | Deliver the service consistently; send agreed reminders and follow-ups; publish two useful clinician-reviewed answers to questions customers actually asked. | Attendance, support time, satisfaction, refunds, appropriate repeat bookings. |
| Days 22–30 | Review the first cohort; keep the channel bringing suitable paying customers; test a small paid campaign only after direct contribution is positive. | Cost per paying customer, contribution, return/referral behaviour. |

Aim for 20 paying customers as an initial learning cohort, not a promised sales
result. Two channels are enough initially: personal referrals/community
introductions and service-specific search content. Use campaign links to tell
which one brings actual bookings. Avoid paying per medical referral; start with
educational partnerships and voluntary introductions.

If NARI has an eligible in-person operation, use an accurate Google Business
Profile. Eligibility depends on a real customer-facing location or travelling
to customers; do not invent a clinic location for an online-only operation.
[Google's Business Profile guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en).
Keep hours, services and details current and request honest customer feedback;
Google describes relevance, distance and prominence as local ranking factors.
[Google local-ranking guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en).

## Weekly scorecard and next product work

Since some customers already come through WhatsApp, make that your first sales
channel to measure. Use the new admin WhatsApp enquiry/order records to count
enquiries, plan choices, payments and completed consultations. At only 3–4
customers, compare the onboarding and monthly provider costs with actual sales
before buying an expensive messaging platform contract. The mobile Business
app cannot run the requested integration on its own. See
[WHATSAPP-SETUP.md](WHATSAPP-SETUP.md) for account prerequisites and limitations.

Track enquiries → bookings → payments → attended consultations, refund count,
clinician cost, support minutes, acquisition cost, contribution, and repeat or
referral customers. A medically appropriate discharge can be a successful
outcome; do not drive unnecessary consultations just to increase retention.
Keep health notes out of marketing spreadsheets and ad events.

At this scale, reliable scheduling and human follow-up matter more than another
app. After the process works, prioritize payment confirmation with a verified
server-side webhook, appointment availability, reminders, and feedback collection.
Avoid spending on broad nationwide ads, expanding cities, or adding unrelated
features until you know which service people buy and what it costs to deliver.
